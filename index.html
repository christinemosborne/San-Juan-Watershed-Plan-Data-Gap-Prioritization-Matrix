Data-gap prioritization matrix

Score each monitoring need or data gap across four factors. Impairment risk and uncertainty are weighted ×2 to reflect implementation priorities. The highest-scoring gaps become first-tier priorities for future monitoring investment and grant development.

Priority score = (Impairment risk ×2) + (Uncertainty ×2) + (Feasibility ×1) + (Regulatory / stakeholder ×1)  ·  1 = low  ·  2 = medium  ·  3 = high  ·  Max score = 12

Tiers: Tier 1 Score 9–12 — first priority for investment & coordination Tier 2 Score 5–8 — address opportunistically or in later phases Tier 3 Score 1–4 — documented; revisit if new information changes importance

Scoring guidance

×2 Impairment risk

Does the gap affect understanding of a listed impairment, a high-risk pollutant pathway, or a subwatershed identified as a likely contributor?

1Indirect or minor connection to any listed impairment
2Moderate connection; pollutant pathway plausible
3Directly affects listed impairment or high-risk pathway
×2 Uncertainty magnitude

How much does the gap limit confidence in source attribution, trend interpretation, pollutant reduction targeting, background interpretation, or BMP selection?

1Minor effect on confidence; alternatives exist
2Meaningful limitation; interpretation is uncertain
3Severely limits source attribution or load targeting
×1 Feasibility

Can monitoring realistically be implemented given access constraints, hydrologic conditions, staffing, cost, timing, and partner capacity?

1Major barriers — access, cost, or staffing constraints
2Achievable with some coordination or resources
3Readily implementable with current capacity
×1 Regulatory / stakeholder

Would filling the gap materially support TMDL or 303(d) work, watershed-plan implementation, grant funding, tribal or community concerns, or cross-jurisdictional coordination?

1Limited regulatory or stakeholder relevance
2Moderate support for one regulatory or stakeholder need
3Directly supports TMDL, grant, or tribal/community need
